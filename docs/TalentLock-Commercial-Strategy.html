Commercial Summary

What the business sells, to whom, on what commercial terms — and an unvarnished view of how close the platform is to collecting revenue.

1.1 The Proposition

TalentLock sells certainty in freelance hiring. It is not a job board and not a staffing agency. It is the transactional system of record for an engagement — from discovery through verification, negotiation, contract, delivery, and retrospective — with exclusivity and verified identity enforced by the platform rather than promised by the counterparty.

The commercial insight is that buyers of specialist freelance talent are not short of candidates; they are short of confidence. The expensive failures in freelance hiring happen after the match: the contractor takes a second engagement mid-project, the credential turns out to be unverified or expired, the contract is an email thread, the scope was never written down, and nobody can say afterwards whether the engagement was actually good value. Marketplaces optimise the match and disengage precisely where the risk begins.

TalentLock monetises that gap. Its differentiated assets are the exclusivity lock (a booked professional is made unavailable by the system, with availability blocks written automatically), bilateral verification (both employer and professional present tiered, AI-adjudicated identity and credential badges, with expiry tracked on a 90/30/7-day alert ladder), and a complete contract lifecycle (AI drafting or upload, redlining, a five-dimension health score, plain-English summarisation, dual e-signature, and a certified PDF) that never requires leaving the platform.

The One-Sentence Pitch

TalentLock is where you hire a specialist and know they are actually yours — verified, exclusively committed, under a signed contract, with the whole engagement recorded in one place instead of scattered across a marketplace, an inbox, and a folder of PDFs.

1.2 What We Are Actually Selling

Commercially it is important to be precise about the unit of value, because it determines how the product is priced and how a sales conversation should be framed. TalentLock sells four distinct things, in descending order of willingness to pay.

SELL 01
Risk removal
Exclusivity enforcement, bilateral verification, credential expiry monitoring, and a signed enforceable agreement for every engagement.
Buyer pays to avoid a failed engagement
SELL 02
Time compression
AI matching, autonomous outreach agents, AI-drafted contracts, generated meeting briefs and proposals — hours of manual work removed per engagement.
Buyer pays to reclaim hiring-manager time
SELL 03
Decision quality
Rate benchmarking against market and historical spend, contract health scoring, hiring funnel analytics, and post-engagement debriefs.
Buyer pays to stop overpaying and re-hiring badly
SELL 04
System of record
One auditable place holding contracts, signatures, credentials, messages, spend, and outcomes across every engagement.
Buyer pays for auditability and continuity

The supply side buys something different and narrower: qualified inbound demand without bidding. Professionals pay to be found, to be visibly verified, and to have an autonomous agent pitch on their behalf while they work — not to browse listings.

Material Commercial Risk — Selling What Is Not Built

The pricing page renders each plan's features array verbatim to prospects. Four entries describe capabilities that do not exist in the codebase:

  • SSO / SCIM (Enterprise) — authentication is Clerk-based; no SAML or SCIM provisioning path exists anywhere in the tree.
  • Audit log export (Enterprise) — an audit_logs table and a platform-admin view exist, but there is no customer-facing export.
  • Priority placement in Vault search (Freelancer Pro) — no ranking logic anywhere reads the subscription plan.
  • Verified Pro badge on profile (Freelancer Pro) — badges are derived from verified documents, not from plan tier.

Two of these sit in the Enterprise tier, which is precisely where they will be written into a contract and diligenced by a security reviewer. Remove or build them before the first enterprise conversation. Of the four, SSO/SCIM is the only one likely to be a genuine deal-breaker for that segment, and it is also the most expensive to deliver.

Market Problem & Positioning

The buyer problem in its own language, the cost of leaving it unsolved, and where TalentLock sits relative to the tools a prospect is already paying for.

2.1 The Trust Gap

Freelance hiring has abundant supply and almost no assurance. The market has solved finding people and left relying on them entirely to the buyer.

A hiring manager engaging a specialist contractor today runs an unmanaged risk portfolio. Five failure modes recur, and none of them are addressed by the platform that made the introduction.

The three "nobody" rows are the commercial opening. They are precisely the failures a buyer has learned to absorb because no vendor has offered to carry them.
Positioning Consequence

Because the differentiation sits after the match, TalentLock should never be sold on the strength of its matching. Every marketplace claims good matching and buyers have learned to discount the claim. Lead instead with the exclusivity lock — it is concrete, demonstrable in thirty seconds, and no incumbent offers it.

2.2 The Cost of the Status Quo

Buyers rarely have a line item called "freelance hiring risk", so the value has to be constructed with them during discovery. The framework below is the arithmetic to walk a prospect through. The inputs come from the prospect — the platform supplies the structure, not the numbers.

Cost Centre How It Is Incurred Today What TalentLock Changes
Failed engagement A contractor is unavailable, under-qualified, or exits mid-project. Cost is the sunk fee plus re-hiring plus the delivery slip. Exclusivity is enforced in the schema and credentials are AI-verified before engagement. The two most common causes are removed structurally.
Hiring-manager time Screening, chasing references, drafting scope, negotiating by email, writing the contract, chasing signatures. AI matching with explanations, AI-drafted contracts, generated meeting briefs, in-platform negotiation and e-signature. Measured in hours per engagement.
Rate leakage No visibility into market rate or the organisation's own historical spend, so each rate is negotiated from zero. Rate benchmarking against market median and the employer's own history, surfaced at the moment of negotiation.
Legal exposure Unreviewed templates, missing IP or termination clauses, unverifiable signature trails. Contract health scored across five dimensions, AI redlining, dual e-signature with stored signature images and timestamps, certified PDF.
Compliance risk Expired professional licences or background checks go unnoticed until an audit or an incident. Expiry tracked with a 90/30/7-day alert ladder; lapsed credentials downgrade the badge automatically and, in the education vertical, remove the profile from the Vault.
Institutional amnesia When the hiring manager leaves, the knowledge of who was good and why leaves with them. Reviews, post-engagement AI debriefs with rehire recommendations, candidate hiring files, and retained spend and outcome analytics.
Discovery Technique — Make The Buyer Do The Arithmetic

The strongest version of this conversation is not a slide. Ask three questions and let the prospect compute the number themselves: "How many contractor engagements did you run last year?" · "How many went wrong enough that you had to re-hire or slip a date?" · "What did one of those cost you, all in?" A single prevented failure typically dwarfs an annual subscription, which reframes the pricing conversation from cost to insurance before pricing is ever discussed.

Positioning Risk — Being Mis-Filed

If a prospect files TalentLock as "another marketplace" it will be benchmarked against a zero-subscription, take-rate model and lose on price. The category to claim is engagement assurance — the system of record for contractor engagements — because that framing carries a compliance-and-risk budget rather than a tools budget. Insist on that frame early; it is very hard to change after the first pricing discussion.

2.5 Defensibility

Feature parity is achievable by a funded competitor. The durable advantages are the ones that compound with usage or would require a rival to rebuild a data model rather than ship a screen.

STRONG Two-sided verified graph
Verification is bilateral and tiered, with expiry state machines on both credentials and teaching licences. A competitor can add document upload in a sprint; accumulating a population of currently verified, non-expired professionals takes calendar time that cannot be compressed.
STRONG Engagement history as a data asset
Rate benchmarking, spend analytics, retention metrics, and rehire recommendations all improve with completed engagements. This is a genuine switching cost: leaving means losing the organisation's own negotiating history and outcome record.
MODERATE Exclusivity as a schema invariant
Copyable in principle, but it constrains a marketplace's core incentive. An incumbent monetising volume through a take rate is structurally reluctant to make its highest-earning supply unavailable. This is a business-model moat more than a technical one.
MODERATE Vertical depth in education
Teaching profession types, subject and level matching, DBS status, and licence-expiry-driven Vault exclusion are modelled in the schema rather than bolted on. A horizontal competitor would need a vertical data model to match it.
WEAK AI features in isolation
Drafting, summarisation, and scoring are commodity capabilities over commodity models. They are valuable as workflow embedding — twenty surfaces sitting exactly where the decision is made — not as differentiators in their own right. Never sell "we have AI".
ABSENT Network effects across employers
Nothing yet makes the platform more valuable to Employer A because Employer B uses it — no shared reputation signal, benchmark pool, or portable trust score. This is the most significant strategic gap and the clearest candidate for the next moat-building investment.

3.1 Two-Sided Dynamics

Both sides are monetised, but asymmetrically and for different reasons. Getting this asymmetry right is the difference between a marketplace that fills and one that stalls.

Recommendation — Do Not Rush To Monetise Supply

Freelancer Pro currently lists at $0 (previously $19). Keeping it free longer is likely the correct commercial choice: supply liquidity is the precondition for every employer deal, and the Vault has a hard quality gate at completenessScore ≥ 60 that already filters low-effort profiles without charging for it. Charging supply too early suppresses the inventory that demand-side revenue depends on. Revisit only once employer demand demonstrably exceeds available verified supply in a given category.

Note also that two of the four Pro benefits currently advertised — priority Vault placement and a Pro badge — are not implemented, so the tier cannot presently be sold with integrity at any price.

3.4 Supply-Side Ideal Profile

Supply quality determines whether demand-side value is deliverable. Two archetypes matter; the platform's gates already select for both.

Independent Specialist
Fits: senior technology or professional-services contractor, works on a small number of substantial engagements, wants inbound demand without competing on price. Signals: completes the profile past the 60% gate, uploads credentials, activates Cruise Mode.
Teaching Professional
Fits: school teacher, university lecturer, tutor, or researcher with a licence and background check to evidence. Signals: supplies teaching subjects and levels, licence expiry, and DBS status — all first-class schema fields rather than free text.
Does Not Fit
High-volume commodity task workers. The exclusivity model actively penalises them — a booking removes their availability — and the verification burden exceeds the value of a small engagement.

3.5 The Education Vertical — Sharpest Wedge

Education is the one segment where TalentLock's compliance machinery is not a differentiator but a requirement. That converts a “nice product” conversation into a “how do you currently prove this” conversation.

Recommendation — Lead With Education

Concentrating early go-to-market on education produces a compounding advantage: it is the segment where the differentiators are mandatory, where the buyer has a named compliance owner with budget, and where a reference customer is most persuasive to the next prospect. Horizontal technology contracting is a larger pool but a much flatter, more crowded sale in which the exclusivity argument competes against price.

3.6 Qualification Framework

A deal is qualified when four conditions hold. Any missing condition predicts a specific, known failure mode.

#Condition Evidence to obtainIf absent
1Recurring specialist demand A number of engagements per quarter, and named roles No renewal case — they will churn after one hire
2A remembered failure A specific engagement that went wrong and its cost No urgency; the deal will slip indefinitely
3Compliance or exclusivity stake Credential requirements, or a double-booking incident Product is over-specified; they will buy a cheaper directory
4Budget owner engaged Founder, COO, or Finance in the conversation Champion cannot buy; expect a stall at procurement

Disqualify early on any of these

  • SSO or SCIM is a stated requirement. It is advertised but not built. Disqualify or scope explicitly as a roadmap commitment with dates — never imply it exists.
  • Escrow or platform payment is required. Payments are entirely out of scope; there is no processor integration.
  • Procurement demands an uptime SLA or penetration test report. Neither exists today; no monitoring is instrumented to support an availability commitment.
  • The buyer wants a take-rate or success-fee model. The product is a subscription; no billing mechanism exists to support transactional pricing.

5.3 Entitlement Gating Map

Three gate functions control premium capability, each currently short-circuited by a single flag.

Commercial Consequence Of The Open Flag

All three gates return true unconditionally while PREMIUM_FEATURES_FREE is set, on both server and client. Every account therefore enjoys Enterprise capability at $0. Three consequences follow:

  1. No upgrade pressure exists. Nobody will ever hit a wall, so no self-serve expansion can occur.
  2. Expectation debt is accumulating. Every current user is habituated to capability they are not paying for. Enabling gates later is a takeaway, and takeaways churn users.
  3. Margin is unprotected. Token ceilings are the only bound on inference cost, and they are disabled — compounded by the absence of any request rate limit on AI endpoints.

Recommendation: enable gating before the user base grows further. The cost of the takeaway rises with every account added.

Margin Assessment

The architecture supports a healthy software gross margin: costs are attributable, results are cached, models are tiered, and the highest-volume automation is budget-capped. The risk is operational, not structural. Once quotas and a rate limit are enabled, the Starter tier's 50,000 monthly tokens becomes a genuine floor on cost per account, and per-feature data already exists to reprice or restrict any capability that proves unprofitable. Until then, cost per account should be monitored directly from token_usage rather than assumed.

5.6 Pricing Recommendations

  1. Reconcile the plan table to plans.ts and publish a single internal pricing sheet. Until this is done, every quote is a guess.
  2. Remove the four unbuilt claims from the pricing page — SSO/SCIM, audit log export, priority Vault placement, and the Pro badge — or build them. Advertising unbuilt capability at the Enterprise tier is the single largest commercial liability in the product today.
  3. Restore Growth to a real price before the user base grows further. The $49 to $199 step is defensible on capability, but only if Growth capability is actually withheld from Starter.
  4. Raise Starter's booking ceiling from 2 to 3. Two concurrent engagements is a very low ceiling for a $49 product and risks reading as artificial rather than protective.
  5. Move team accounts from Enterprise to Growth with three seats included and per-seat pricing above that, converting the Growth-to-Enterprise cliff into a ramp.
  6. Keep Freelancer Pro free until verified supply exceeds employer demand, and remove its two unbuilt benefits immediately regardless.
  7. Express token limits as outcomes in all customer-facing material, never as raw token counts.
  8. Do not introduce a take rate. No payment rail exists, and the absence of a percentage cut is a decisive differentiator against staffing agencies — it should be defended as a positioning asset, not treated as unrealised revenue.

Sales Enablement

The narrative, the questions that qualify, the objections that will come, and what can be claimed with evidence.

6.1 The Narrative Arc

Four moves. The purpose of each is to make the next one feel inevitable rather than to describe software.

MOVE 1 Name the failure they have already lived
“Most teams we speak to have had a contractor go quiet or take another project mid-delivery. Has that happened to you?” This is deliberately not a product statement. It establishes that the problem being solved is one they recognise, and it surfaces the specific incident that becomes the business case.
MOVE 2 Locate the gap in the tools they already pay for
“The marketplace introduced them, the e-signature tool signed something, the spreadsheet tracked the invoice. Which of those stopped them taking a second engagement, or told you their licence had expired?” The answer is none. This reframes TalentLock as filling a hole rather than replacing an incumbent — which protects it from a like-for-like price comparison.
MOVE 3 Demonstrate the lock
Book someone live. The Vault updates, the availability block appears, and it cannot be removed. Fifteen seconds, no narration required. This is the moment the category shifts in the buyer's mind from “another hiring tool” to “engagement assurance”.
MOVE 4 Hand over the ledger
Spend by field, rate versus market, funnel conversion, retention, rehire recommendations. This is the move that converts a hiring-manager enthusiasm into a Finance-approved subscription, and it is what makes renewal a formality rather than a re-sale.

6.3 Objection Handling

Each response below is grounded in a capability that genuinely exists, or concedes the gap plainly. Do not improvise past a gap — three of these objections concern things that are not built, and a confident wrong answer will surface in diligence.

We already use a freelance marketplace and it's cheaper.
Agree, and separate the jobs. A marketplace introduces people; it does not stop the person you hired taking a parallel engagement, verify that a licence is still valid, draft or score your contract, or tell you afterwards whether the engagement was good value. Also compare total cost honestly — a percentage take rate on engagement value will typically exceed a flat subscription well before the year is out.
Evidence: exclusivity lock · expiry ladder · contract health score · no take rate
Our agency handles vetting for us.
Agencies do vet well — and charge a margin on every hour for the privilege, while the vetting evidence stays with them rather than with you. TalentLock gives you the verification artefact and the audit trail directly, at a flat subscription, with the professional relationship held by you rather than intermediated.
Evidence: AI document verification · tiered badges · audit logging · flat pricing
How is your AI matching better than everyone else's?
Don't fight on this ground — every vendor claims it and buyers discount it. Redirect: “Matching is table stakes and I'd rather not compete on adjectives. What nobody else does is make the person unavailable to everyone else once you book them, and tell you when their licence is about to expire. Can I show you that instead?”
Tactic: concede the commodity, pivot to the differentiator
Do you handle payments or escrow?
No — concede immediately and cleanly. There is no payment rail and no processor integration. Position it as deliberate scope: TalentLock governs the engagement — verification, exclusivity, contract, outcome — and leaves invoicing to the systems finance already trusts. If escrow is a hard requirement, this is a genuine disqualifier today.
Gap — do not imply a roadmap date that has not been committed
We need SSO. Our security policy requires it.
Not built. Say so. Authentication is handled by a managed identity provider, but there is no SAML or SCIM provisioning path. If SSO is mandatory, either disqualify or scope it as an explicit, dated roadmap commitment with engineering sign-off — never as an existing feature, even though the pricing page currently lists it.
Gap — pricing page claim must be corrected before enterprise conversations
What's your uptime commitment?
There is no contractual SLA today, and no monitoring instrumented to support one. Be straightforward about that and offer what does exist: an autoscaling deployment, a continuous integration suite covering the API surface, and a documented security posture. Do not invent a number.
Gap — no APM, tracing, or uptime metrics are wired
This looks like a lot of product. Will my team actually use it?
Fair challenge — narrow it. The daily surface is small: search, book, sign. The rest is either automatic (verification, expiry alerts, briefs, debriefs generate themselves) or reporting the buyer looks at monthly. Frame adoption around the three-step core and let the automation reveal itself.
Evidence: fire-and-forget automation · briefs and debriefs generated without user action
Why is your $199 tier free right now?
Answer honestly: premium gating is temporarily open while billing is finalised. Then use it — this is a legitimate reason to sign now, and a real deadline. Do not pretend the list price is being charged; the prospect can see $0 on the pricing page.
Context: PREMIUM_FEATURES_FREE is enabled; Growth and Pro list at $0
Who else in our sector uses this?
There are no named references yet. Do not manufacture them. Offer verifiable substance instead — a live demonstration on their own requirement, the specification and test coverage behind the product, and a pilot structured around one real engagement so they generate their own proof.
Gap — no customer references exist; pilots substitute for social proof

6.5 Competitive Traps

  • Being drawn into a matching bake-off. Matching quality is unprovable in a demo and every competitor claims it. Concede it as table stakes and move to exclusivity.
  • Being priced against a take rate. Insist on total-cost-of-engagement arithmetic; a percentage of engagement value overtakes a flat subscription quickly.
  • Letting an agency reframe verification as service. The counter is ownership: with an agency the vetting evidence and the relationship both stay with the agency.
  • Demonstrating gated features to a Starter prospect. Trivially easy today because gates are open, and it manufactures a downgrade experience at contract time.
  • Accepting an SSO requirement to keep a deal alive. It will fail security review. Losing the deal at qualification is far cheaper than losing it at diligence.
  • Promising payment or escrow “soon”. No rail exists and none is scoped. This creates a commitment the roadmap has not accepted.

Claims Discipline

A single reference for what customer-facing teams may state as fact, what must be qualified, and what must not be said at all.

StatementVerdict Required framing
Booking makes a professional exclusively unavailable SAY IT Demonstrate live; it is enforced in the data model
Identity and credentials are AI-verified with human escalation SAY IT Accurate; low-confidence results route to an admin review queue
Expiring licences trigger staged alerts SAY IT 90/30/7-day ladder; expired education licences leave the Vault
Contracts are drafted, scored, signed and issued as PDF in-platform SAY IT Complete lifecycle; no external e-signature tool required
Spend and rate benchmarking are available SAY IT Against market median and the customer's own history
Multi-currency hiring is supported SAY IT Booking currency frozen with an FX snapshot at creation
Purpose-built for education hiring SAY IT Profession types, subject/level matching, DBS, licence expiry, session rates
“Enterprise-ready” QUALIFY Name the controls that exist. Do not use the phrase as a shorthand — it implies SSO and an SLA, neither of which exist
Plan limits and prices QUALIFY Quote only from plans.ts. Growth and Pro currently list at $0
Premium features included QUALIFY Currently open to all plans by configuration. State that it is temporary, in writing
SSO, SCIM, audit log export DO NOT Not implemented. Remove from the pricing page; never quote
Priority Vault placement, Verified Pro badge DO NOT No ranking or badge logic reads the plan
Payments, escrow, invoicing DO NOT No payment rail exists in any form
Uptime or availability figures DO NOT Nothing is instrumented to measure or substantiate a number
Customer counts, logos, references, case studies DO NOT None exist. Use pilots so the customer generates their own evidence
Penetration test or certification status DO NOT No third-party assessment has been performed
Standing Instruction

Four capabilities are currently advertised on the live pricing page and are not implemented: SSO/SCIM, audit log export, priority Vault placement, and the Verified Pro badge. Until the pricing page is corrected, treat everything rendered there as unverified and quote exclusively from this document's Appendix B. If a prospect raises one of these four, correct the record immediately — an early, honest correction costs a feature; a late one discovered in diligence costs the deal and the relationship.
