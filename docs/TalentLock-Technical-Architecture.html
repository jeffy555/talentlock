Executive Summary & Application Overview

What the platform does, who it serves, why the architecture is shaped the way it is, and the measurable state of the system on the main branch.

1.1 Platform Thesis

TalentLock is a vertically integrated freelance engagement platform that compresses the entire hiring lifecycle — discovery, verification, negotiation, contracting, execution, and retrospective — into a single transactional system of record.

Conventional talent marketplaces optimise for listing volume: they match supply to demand and withdraw once contact is established, leaving contracting, exclusivity, and compliance to email and PDF attachments. TalentLock inverts that model. Its central architectural premise is the exclusivity lock — a booked freelancer is atomically marked unavailable, availability blocks are auto-generated, and the engagement is bound to a machine-generated, dual-signed legal agreement before work begins. Every downstream feature is a consequence of that premise.

The second organising premise is that AI is infrastructure, not a feature. Twenty discrete inference surfaces are embedded directly in domain workflows — document verification, agreement drafting, contract health scoring, rate benchmarking, interview briefing, post-engagement debriefing, and two autonomous outreach agents. Each is metered through a single accounting seam (token_usage) so cost attribution is a first-class property of the schema rather than an afterthought.

Architectural Consequence

Because exclusivity and contract state are authoritative in Postgres — not in a third-party document service — the system requires strict transactional discipline. Quota enforcement, verification-level recalculation, and completeness scoring all execute inside Drizzle transactions using SELECT … FOR UPDATE row locks. Conversely, every non-authoritative side effect (notifications, email, AI generation, analytics) is dispatched fire-and-forget and can never delay or fail a user-facing write.

Architectural Constraint — Isolated Admin Trust Domain

The administrative principal never traverses Clerk. It authenticates against ADMIN_USERNAME/ADMIN_PASSWORD and receives an HMAC-SHA256 signed tl_admin cookie with an 8-hour TTL, signed with SESSION_SECRET. All mutating admin routes are additionally protected by a csrf-csrf double-submit token fetched from GET /api/admin/csrf-token. Login is rate-limited to 10 attempts per 5 minutes per IP, and fails closed: if ADMIN_PASSWORD is unset, admin login is disabled entirely rather than defaulting open.

1.3 Core Value Proposition

Exclusivity as a Primitive
A confirmed booking atomically flips freelancer availability, writes an availability_blocks row, and gates agreement generation until both parties converge on rate. Exclusivity is enforced in the schema, not by convention.
Verifiable Trust
Bilateral verification: freelancers submit identity and credential documents, employers submit business registration. Both produce a tiered badge recalculated atomically on every status change, with credential expiry tracked on a 90/30/7-day alert ladder.
Contract Lifecycle Ownership
Agreements are AI-drafted or employer-uploaded, redlined, health-scored across five dimensions, plain-English summarised, dual-signed with stored signature images, and emitted as a formatted PDF — without leaving the platform.
Autonomous Demand Matching
Two symmetric background agents — Cruise Mode (freelancer-side) and TalentSearch (employer-side) — evaluate every new job post or profile update and dispatch AI-authored direct messages under strict rate, budget, and duplicate ceilings.

2.2 Primary Workflow — Employer Engagement Lifecycle

The canonical revenue path: from talent discovery to a dual-signed, downloadable agreement and post-engagement retrospective.

Registration & business verification
Account type selection, then a single consolidated form capturing location (deriving currency), company profile, and a mandatory Representative ID upload. The document is queued for AI vision review; verificationLevel is recalculated atomically on every status change.
PATCH /users/me/onboarding-step → PUT /employers/me → POST /employer-documents/confirm → PUT /users/me
Talent discovery — three parallel modalities
Deterministic filtering in the Talent Vault (field, rate, availability window, keyword, profession category, teaching subject); conversational AI matching that reads job requirements and recommends candidates with explanations; or fully autonomous TalentSearch that evaluates profiles as they change. Vault visibility requires completenessScore ≥ 60.
GET /freelancers?q=&availableFrom= · POST /openai/conversations/:id/messages · GET /ai/match-explanation
Pipeline curation
Candidates are saved to a personal watchlist with private notes, plan-limited to 25 (Starter) or 100 (Growth). Active enterprise team members are redirected server-side to the shared team_shortlist, receiving 403/[] on personal endpoints. Watchlist entries emit debounced alerts on availability or ≥5% rate change.
POST /freelancers/:id/save · PATCH /freelancers/:id/watchlist · GET /freelancers/saved
Discovery meeting & AI brief
A meeting request transitions to confirmed, which fire-and-forget triggers a five-section AI brief — candidate snapshot, match rationale, suggested questions, rate context, watch points — cached as briefContent jsonb. The brief is employer-only and never exposed to the freelancer.
POST /meetings → PATCH /meetings/:id {status:confirmed} ↝ generateMeetingBrief()
Hiring disposition
After completion the employer submits internal notes with a disposition. next_round produces an AI handoff summary routed to a team member or panel email; proceed_to_booking unlocks the Book CTA and upserts a candidate hiring file; rejected records the file only. One submission per meeting — 409 thereafter.
POST /meetings/:id/feedback {disposition} → employer_candidate_notes
Booking creation & rate convergence
Booking creation acquires a FOR UPDATE lock on the employer row to evaluate the active-booking ceiling before insert. Currency is frozen at creation alongside an exchangeRateAtCreation snapshot. Rate negotiation alternates proposals until negotiationStatus converges; agreement generation stays gated until then.
POST /bookings → POST /bookings/:id/negotiate · POST /ai/rate-suggestion
Agreement construction — generated or uploaded
Either GPT-4o drafts a contract from booking particulars and industry clause templates, or the employer uploads PDF/DOCX/TXT which is text-extracted and AI-summarised. The upload path advances summary_ready → enriched → finalized; signing is blocked until finalized.
POST /agreements  |  POST /agreements/upload-url → upload-confirm → PATCH :id/amendments → POST :id/enrich → POST :id/finalize
Contract assurance
On-demand redlining proposes edits; a health score grades five dimensions (Clarity, Fairness, Completeness, Enforceability, Industry Fit) to a 0–100 composite and A–F letter. Accepting a redline atomically invalidates both the health score and freelancer summary caches in a single update.
POST /agreements/:id/redline → PATCH :id/accept-redline → POST :id/health-score
Execution & certified artefact
Both parties sign using stored signature images or typed cursive names. At fully_signed a formatted PDF is rendered via @react-pdf/renderer with both signatures, timestamps, and metadata, then cached to object storage on a fire-and-forget upload. Download is 403 unless fully signed.
POST /agreements/:id/sign → GET /agreements/:id/download
Delivery, closure & retrospective
Milestones track title, amount, due date, and status. On transition to completed a dual-perspective AI debrief is generated fire-and-forget; each party reads only their role-filtered slice. The employer then submits a star rating and comment, which the freelancer may answer once.
PATCH /bookings/:id {status:completed} ↝ generateBookingDebrief() → GET /bookings/:id/debrief · POST /reviews

2.3 Primary Workflow — Freelancer Supply Lifecycle

Profile establishment & completeness gate
Registration captures work category, location, professional details, and a mandatory identity document. An optional résumé import pre-fills fields but never satisfies the document gate. calculateCompletenessScore() is written in the same update as every profile change; below 60 the profile is invisible in the Talent Vault.
PATCH /freelancers/me · POST /freelancers/parse-resume · POST /documents/confirm
Credential verification & expiry stewardship
Documents receive GPT-4o vision adjudication returning verified | rejected | needs_review with a confidence score; low confidence escalates to the admin queue. Verified counts drive a three-tier badge. A daily scan advances an expiryAlertStage ladder (90d → 30d → 7d → expired) that only ever moves forward, making the scan idempotent and safe to miss a day.
POST /documents/confirm ↝ triggerDocumentReview() · PATCH /documents/:type/expiry
Availability & portfolio curation
Manual blocks (holiday, unavailable) coexist with booking-derived auto-blocks, which are immutable — deletion returns 409. nextAvailableDate is recomputed and surfaced on Vault cards and the ?availableFrom= filter. Portfolio items and a public /f/:id profile support off-platform discovery.
POST /availability/me · DELETE /availability/me/:id · POST /portfolio
Demand capture — inbound and autonomous
Express interest in open postings (plan-limited, FOR UPDATE guarded), or delegate to Cruise Mode: rules are declared via form or free-form text parsed by AI, then every new job post is evaluated and an AI-authored DM sent on the freelancer's behalf above threshold — capped at 10 messages monthly with blackout windows and a dry-run mode.
POST /job-requirements/:id/interest · POST /cruise-mode/parse-rules → PATCH /cruise-mode/activate
Negotiation & contract comprehension
An AI proposal generator drafts responses in three tones. On the agreement, a freelancer-only summariser produces a six-section plain-English digest with up to three attention flags, disclaimer first. The endpoint returns 403 for employers and the panel is not rendered at all for them.
POST /ai/proposal · POST /agreements/:id/summarise · POST /agreements/:id/sign
Earnings intelligence
A six-month earnings trend against platform average, rate benchmarking percentile, forward monthly projection, and top-earning skills — all derived from completed bookings in the freelancer's own currency.
GET /dashboard/earnings-intelligence

Technical Note — The 402 Contract

Commercial refusals are expressed as HTTP 402 Payment Required with a uniform body { error, code, planNeeded }. The client discriminates on code: TOKEN_LIMIT renders an inline error inside the originating drawer or panel, while PLAN_LIMIT redirects to /pricing. 3.1 Structural Pattern Analysis

TalentLock is a Modular Monolith deployed as two processes — a static single-page client and a single Express service — over one PostgreSQL database, organised inside a pnpm workspace monorepo with a contract-first API boundary.

CONFIRMED Modular Monolith with enforced internal seams
All 179 handlers execute in one process against one database. Modularity is achieved through directory and package boundaries rather than network boundaries: 40 route modules delegate to 66 single-responsibility library modules, and cross-cutting concerns are extracted into named seams (accessControl.ts, subscriptionGating.ts, tokenLogger.ts, createNotification.ts, objectStorage.ts). Route handlers orchestrate; they do not implement.
CONFIRMED Contract-first API boundary
lib/api-spec/openapi.yaml (9,316 lines) is the single source of truth. Orval generates React Query hooks into lib/api-client-react/ and Zod validators into lib/api-zod/ from that one artefact. The client cannot call an endpoint the contract does not describe, and the server validates against schemas derived from the same document — client/server drift is structurally impossible, not merely discouraged.
CONFIRMED Layered request pipeline
A uniform vertical sequence — boundary middleware → route handler → authorisation → validation → entitlement → domain library → ORM → deferred egress. Every handler follows it, which is why the codebase reads consistently across 48 feature domains.
CONFIRMED Deferred side-effect (fire-and-forget) model
Authoritative state commits synchronously; all derived effects — notifications, email, AI generation, audit logs, analytics, PDF caching — dispatch with .catch() and are never awaited. This is the system's substitute for a message broker: it delivers responsiveness and failure isolation without introducing queue infrastructure.
REJECTED Microservices
No service mesh, inter-service RPC, or per-service datastore. Given a single team, a shared relational core with heavy cross-entity joins (bookings ↔ agreements ↔ meetings ↔ profiles), and strict transactional invariants spanning those entities, distribution would impose distributed-transaction cost for no organisational benefit.
REJECTED Serverless / function-per-endpoint
The application is a long-lived Express process. Notably, the deployment target's autoscale idling forced scheduled work out of process — the credential-expiry scan is driven by an external GitHub Actions cron rather than an in-process timer.
PARTIAL Clean / Hexagonal Architecture
Ports-and-adapters discipline is applied selectively where substitution has real value: objectStorage.ts abstracts Azure Blob, GCS, and local disk behind one interface. Elsewhere the ORM and framework types reach into domain modules, so this is a pragmatic partial adoption rather than a strict dependency-inversion architecture.
Technical Note — Why This Pattern Fits

The dominant architectural forces here are transactional integrity (exclusivity locks, quota ceilings, verification-level recalculation) and contract stability across a large, fast-growing API surface. A modular monolith with a generated contract addresses both directly. The two genuine scaling seams — AI inference and document processing — are already decoupled by the fire-and-forget model and could be extracted to workers without disturbing the request path.

Observation — No Global Rate Limit

Rate limiting exists in exactly two places: admin login (10 per 5 minutes per IP) and human direct messages (30 per hour per user per conversation). There is no application-wide limiter on the Clerk-authenticated API surface, including the AI endpoints. Token quotas bound cost per account but not request rate, and those quotas are presently bypassed by PREMIUM_FEATURES_FREE. An authenticated client can therefore drive inference endpoints as fast as OpenAI will answer. A global limiter in front of /api/ai/*, /api/openai/*, and the agreement AI routes would close this before paid launch.

Observability

  • Structured logging — Pino via req.log in every handler; console.log is prohibited by convention. Sensitive fields are redacted at the logger, not at call sites.
  • Decision ledgers — both autonomous agents append an activity row for every outcome, including refusals, making agent behaviour auditable without log archaeology.
  • Cost attribution — token_usage supports per-user, per-feature, and per-conversation rollups, surfaced in both the user billing view and the admin console.
  • Audit trail — audit_logs records authentication events and sensitive mutations with request provenance.
  • Gap — no APM, distributed tracing, error aggregation, or uptime metrics are wired. Diagnosis currently depends on log inspection and database queries.

Architectural Constraint — Fail-Closed Secrets

Three secrets deliberately fail closed rather than degrading to an open state: ADMIN_PASSWORD (admin login disabled), CRON_SECRET (cron route returns 500), and CSRF_SECRET (admin mutations rejected). This is the correct posture — an unconfigured deployment is inert, not publicly writable.

Technical Note — Integration Gating

Every integration suite is wrapped in describe.skipIf(!integrationEnvReady()), which requires a real DATABASE_URL (a placeholder value is explicitly detected and rejected) plus Clerk credentials. Admin sub-suites additionally require ADMIN_PASSWORD. The consequence: a local pnpm test reports 99 passing and 185 skipped. Full-surface verification only occurs in the nightly and main-push CI job where secrets are present — worth knowing before treating a green local run as comprehensive.

Closing Assessment

TalentLock presents as an unusually disciplined codebase for its feature density: 48 domains, 179 endpoints, and 20 metered inference surfaces held together by a generated API contract, a uniform request pipeline, and consistent transactional and deferred-effect patterns. The architecture is coherent and the invariants are documented and enforced rather than merely asserted. The open items are operational rather than structural — rate limiting, runtime instrumentation, one metering omission, and commercial activation. None require architectural change; all are additive to the existing seams.
